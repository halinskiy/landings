liberdynamics.com ↗

“Reels-first. Thumbnails are noise.”

The story

Liberdynamics make reels, shorts, and TikToks for B2B SaaS. Their old site filed everything into a polite three-by-three grid of thumbnails. Three years of award-winning work was being judged by a grid.

We started by deleting the grid. The new home page is one continuous vertical reel: muted, autoplaying, scroll-scrubbed. The first twelve seconds carry the case for the studio. By second twenty, the visitor is reading the metric overlay. By second forty, they are on the contact form.

On the build side, every case study is a Webflow CMS entry with a single thirty-second cut as the hero, a B-roll as the runner-up, and a written paragraph that lives between them. Editors paste one Loom link and the page assembles itself.

What shipped
  1. 01Reels-first home: no grid, no carousel, one continuous scroll-scrubbed cut
  2. 02Case study template: hero cut + B-roll + one paragraph + metric overlay
  3. 03Webflow CMS with one-Loom-link editor flow for new case studies
  4. 04Hardware-accelerated video pipeline tested on iPhone 11 over hotel wifi

noldio.com ↗

“It reads like a deposition, not a brochure.”

The story

Noldio advise enterprise companies on data privacy. Their old site was a wall of corporate language and PDF whitepapers behind a gated form. The brief was simple: make us sound like the room our partners are actually in, not the room our agency thinks we are in.

We rewrote the home page as a one-page argument. Each section sets up a position the visitor already half-believes, then closes it. The page reads like a deposition: claim, evidence, citation, next claim. No carousel, no testimonial wall, no animated counter.

The CMS now hosts long-form essays under the same skeleton: lede, three turns, payoff, citations footer. Every essay routes back to two call-to-action spots, both of which open a Calendly directly, no form.

What shipped
  1. 01One-page argument hero: claim, evidence, citation, repeat
  2. 02Long-read CMS skeleton: lede + three turns + payoff + footnotes
  3. 03Calendly-first CTAs, no gated PDF, no contact form
  4. 04Schema for legal advisory pages tuned to citation by Perplexity and ChatGPT

solutech.global ↗

“Loads before the office modem finishes connecting.”

The story

Solutech run enterprise IT projects across three continents. Their site was a beautiful museum-piece that took nine seconds to paint a hero and pegged a low-end phone's CPU. Their buyers were sales engineers on bad hotel wifi.

We rebuilt the marketing site as a Webflow project tuned to a strict performance budget: 100 KB JS, 60 KB CSS, hero painted from preloaded WebP, no above-the-fold video. The whole site loads inside one second on a 4G connection at Heathrow.

We did not soften the design to get there. The hero still has a typographic moment, the case studies still have B-roll, the testimonials still have faces. The savings came from the build, not from removing the things the design depended on.

What shipped
  1. 01Performance budget enforced at the section level, not the page level
  2. 02Hero hand-painted as preloaded WebP, not a video, not a Lottie
  3. 03Webflow IX2 used sparingly, no scroll-jacked carousels
  4. 04DevOps handover: a one-page runbook so the in-house team can ship pages without us

patientpartner.com ↗

“Twelve fewer steps. Twelve more answers.”

The story

Patient Partner connect newly diagnosed patients with people who have lived the same condition. The flow that introduced a new patient to a peer was twenty-six steps, eleven screens, and three modal dialogs. Most patients gave up after step seven.

We rebuilt the onboarding as one continuous Webflow page with conditional sections, four explicit decision points, and a single back button that always works. The peer match happens before the visitor has typed their phone number. Phone number arrives last, when the visitor is already attached to a face.

Accessibility was the constraint, not the bonus. Every state is reachable by keyboard, every error is announced, every animation has a static fallback. The audit caught twenty-three issues on the old site and zero on the new one.

What shipped
  1. 01Twenty-six step flow rebuilt as a five-decision flow
  2. 02Match before identification: peer is shown before the phone number is asked
  3. 03WCAG 2.2 AA verified on every state with a screen reader pass
  4. 04Memberstack-backed accounts with a one-tap re-entry from email
